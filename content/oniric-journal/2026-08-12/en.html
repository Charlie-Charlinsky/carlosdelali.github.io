Dreams - 12 August 2026

Part I

I am at a boxing training session coordinated by my former coach and my friend Luján. I was doing pad work with a guy who fought very well. I kept putting my gloves back on because they were too big for me. Luján asked philosophical questions about the relationships between tango and fighting movements. When we leave, Luján goes to the bar next door and begins singing to the waitress he is in love with.

Part II

At my house in Las Palas, my mother has a pair of green hair dryers switched on, each inside a cloth bag. Afraid that the bags might burn, I switch off the dryers using the button that can be felt from outside the greenish cloth bag. My mother says that dreaming of a hair dryer switched on inside a cloth bag foretells problems with women. This comment struck me deeply because, at that moment, I was dreaming, and my mother was the interpreter of the dream itself while I was dreaming. What I doubt is whether that statement is symbolically true. After leaving my house in Las Palas, I go to a large nearby town.

The protagonist in this part of the dream is a guy who is dating a girl from the town. The girl shows the videos and short films they recorded together. In one of them, the rear part of a car can be seen. Above the parcel shelf behind the seats that conceals the boot, their heads float, held by strings as if they were balloons. Their faces are those of two mimes. They try to kiss and bite each other, their heads colliding and bouncing off one another and the back of the car. Suddenly another short film appears on the cinema screen. This short is about a man from the town who wants to take the girl away from this guy. It is exactly the same rear part of the car, but this man is a strange, deformed clay doll that vomits pieces of coloured clay. As I leave through a courtyard, I head towards a building. While crossing this courtyard, a small, very black cat latches onto my hand, biting me very hard. Completely wrapped around my hand. The bite and the pain from its teeth and claws gripping me like a centipede are piercing. I grab the cat, pull it off my hand and throw it far away from me. When I enter the building, a team of workers for sports and combat entertainment welcome me. Every time I go to ask for a tracksuit, they give me a green tracksuit. Trousers and sweatshirt. This repeats in a loop until I wake up.

Analysis

This dream has a very unusual feature: it contains its own interpretation within the dream. Your mother does not merely appear as a character; she takes on the role of interpreter and formulates a thesis —“hair dryers switched on inside bags = problems with women”— while you yourself, still dreaming, question the interpretation.

And I think you are right to question it. I specifically searched for hair dryer, hair dryers, bag, bags and the corresponding combination in Emilio Salas’s book, and that specific equivalence does not exist. We therefore should not take that phrase as a symbolic rule coming from the book. Salas himself also warns that the dictionary can only provide general symbols and that many elements are personal; he recommends not entering a dream with a preconceived interpretation.

What is fascinating is that the dream itself seems to test your mother’s statement after she says it.

Part I — Boxing, tango and love

The first part seems very different from the second, but in reality it establishes the fundamental theme.

You are training, not actually fighting. Salas groups fights, combat and wars as representations of inner conflicts or emotional tensions. Here, however, confrontation is regulated: there is training, technique, pad work, a partner, a coach. In other words, aggression is domesticated through discipline.

And the first fundamental detail appears: the gloves do not fit you.

They are too big and you constantly have to readjust them. The book gives great importance to the condition and suitability of gloves, because they are the “clothing of the hand”; it even associates uncomfortable gloves used to perform a task with insecurity regarding one’s own abilities. But I think there is something even more precise here if we connect this entry with the one for hand: for Salas, the hand represents our means of action, our capacity to intervene in reality.

Therefore:

hand → capacity to act
glove → instrument that mediates that action
glove too large → the instrument, function or role still does not completely fit you

And notice that you do not give up. You keep readjusting it and continue fighting. There is no helplessness, but calibration.

Then Luján asks that extremely strange and magnificent question about the relationship between tango and fighting movements. To me, that is where the dream practically formulates its own thesis.

Boxing and tango appear to be opposites, but physically they share a great deal:

distance, approach, withdrawal, anticipation, rhythm, reading the other person, contact, bodily deception, response and synchronisation.

One transforms that coordination into confrontation; the other, into encounter.

In addition, Salas gives dance a strong sentimental and erotic content. And immediately after philosophising about dance + combat, Luján leaves the gym, enters the bar and sings to the woman he is in love with. The book relates singing to the externalisation of sorrow or an intimate feeling seeking expression.

The sequence is quite elegant:

combat → reflection on dance → love → expression through song.

The aggressive energy does not disappear: it is transformed into relationship.

And that same mixture of attraction and aggression will dominate Part II.

Part II — The hair dryers: containing an energy before it burns something

We have two hair dryers running inside two greenish cloth bags.

I cannot find a specific entry for the hair dryer or the bag in Salas, so here we must interpret the scene through how it functions rather than invent an equivalence.

A hair dryer produces hot air. Inside a flammable bag, that potentially useful energy becomes a risk because it is enclosed where it should not be. You immediately understand the danger and switch them off before anything happens.

This fits extraordinarily well with what happened in the gym: once again an energy appears that needs regulation.

In boxing you regulate force through technique.
Here you regulate heat through the switch.

You are not the victim of danger in either scene: you detect it and act.

And there is a second extremely important element: green. In the book, green is especially ambivalent. It is associated with Venus, nature, sensitivity, fertility and perception, but also with that which is still “green”, that is, unfinished, uncertain, immature or needing development before it can become real.

This will be decisive because green reappears at the end of the dream.

Your mother interprets the dream within the dream itself

Something psychologically fascinating happens here.

Your mother says:

dreaming of a hair dryer switched on inside a cloth bag foretells problems with women.

But you do not accept the statement. On the contrary: you realise that you are dreaming and question the interpreter from inside the dream itself.

That is, at least during that fragment, a form of lucid awareness: you do not need to control the dream in order to recognise that you are dreaming.

But I would not interpret your mother as a prophetess introducing true information. I would treat her as an internal voice of authority proposing a hypothesis.

And then something very peculiar happens: the rest of the dream begins to speak precisely about women, couples, jealousy, rivalry and finally a black cat.

It is as though the dream were saying:

“Problems with women? All right. Let us see what that idea means.”

Not prove it: dramatise it.

Because immediately afterwards we literally enter a film.

The mimes: love turned into representation

You arrive in the town and a girl screens the films she has made with her boyfriend.

This is relevant because Salas has a specific entry for theatre, cinema and television: he argues that these settings allow an external plot to be used to represent and update inner problems or experiences; he also considers it relevant whether we are actors, directors, filmmakers or spectators.

And you are a spectator.

You do not participate in the romantic triangle. You observe it transformed into fiction.

The image of the two mime heads is probably one of the dream’s central images.

They have no bodies. Only the faces remain, suspended by threads like balloons. They are also mimes: characters whose identity is built through gesture, mask and silence.

And they do something contradictory:

they try to kiss and bite each other at the same time.

Exactly the thesis from Part I reappears here.

The kiss seeks union.
The bite seeks possession/aggression.

Tango and boxing again.

The heads try to approach each other, collide, bounce away and try again. They cannot establish stable contact. There is attraction, aggression and pendular movement.

I would read this image as a grotesque representation of romantic dynamics when desire and conflict become mixed. Not necessarily of your specific relationship or any particular woman. The fact that everything is projected on a screen deliberately distances the content from you.

And they are also heads without bodies: the relationship has been reduced to representation, gesture and identity. It almost looks like a caricature of “being a couple”.

The clay rival

Then the man who wants to take the girl away from the guy appears.

But the dream does not represent him as a person. It turns him into a deformed clay doll that vomits fragments of coloured clay.

Here the book does offer a useful association: mouldable mud or clay represents material capable of creation and transformation; modelling figures is symbolically equivalent to creating something and giving it form.

But this character does the opposite.

He does not model the clay.

He vomits it.

And he himself is poorly modelled.

That makes me think of something unintegrated, poorly formed or still unable to acquire a coherent form. Rather than representing “a real rival”, he seems like a caricature of rivalry itself: a grotesque figure manufactured by the imagination and continually expelling the same matter of which it is made.

In addition, he expels coloured clay: emotional matter that is still malleable appears once again.

The black cat: here the connection with “problems with women” does appear

And now we reach the strange point.

After your mother has said that the hair dryer means “problems with women”, a completely black cat appears.

In El gran libro de los sueños, Salas traditionally identifies the cat with the feminine, particularly with its sensual and instinctive dimension. And he specifically attributes romantic problems, jealousy and conflicts involving a woman to the black cat.

So yes: the dream later ends up generating a symbol that, within Salas’s system, corresponds much more closely to what your mother had said.

But that does not make the equivalence “hair dryer + bag = problems with women” true.

In fact, I think something much more interesting is happening:

the dream introduces an interpretation and then manufactures images compatible with it.

It is almost a small dream experiment about interpretation itself.

And the cat does not merely appear.

It bites your hand.

Two very powerful entries converge here. Salas defines the bite as attack and appropriation; when the one biting is an animal, he relates it to the fear of becoming dominated by the instincts or passions symbolised by that animal.

And remember:

hand = means of action.

Therefore, the complete scene would be:

an instinctive/feminine force violently clings to your capacity to act.

But what do you do?

You are not paralysed.

You grab the cat, tear it away from your hand and throw it far away.

That radically changes the value of the scene.

The content is not “a woman will cause you problems”.

The narrative content is:

something instinctive/aggressive tries to take possession of your capacity to act → you recover control and remove it.

That pattern has already happened twice before: gloves you must adjust; hair dryers you must switch off.

The green tracksuit and the final loop

After pushing the cat away, you enter another environment connected to sports and combat entertainment.

With this, the dream closes the circle that began in the gym.

At the beginning:

combat training → gloves that still do not fit you.

At the end:

professional combat environment → clothing assigned to you again and again.

And that clothing is green.

Salas’s entry on clothing says that clothes can express both the image we want to project socially and the way we unconsciously see ourselves; when linked to a profession, they take on a particularly strong meaning of identity and social/professional adaptation.

But your professional uniform is green: precisely the colour of what still needs to mature.

And the dream repeats it in a loop.

You ask for clothes.
They give you the green tracksuit.
You ask again.
They give you the same green tracksuit.
Again.

It does not seem like a failure. It seems like an identity that is still being assigned and reassigned without becoming completely fixed.

It is very similar to the oversized gloves.

At one end of the dream you have equipment that does not quite fit.

At the other, equipment that is still green.

That seems much more structural to me than the supposed “omen about women”.

The deep structure of the dream

I think the word that best summarises the whole dream is adjustment.

You are continuously regulating relationships between opposing forces:

force ↔ control
aggression ↔ affection
boxing ↔ tango
biting ↔ kissing
heat ↔ containment
instinct ↔ will
professional role ↔ identity still incomplete

And there is one element that runs through both parts and that I consider the true hidden symbol of the dream: the hand.

First you cannot get the gloves to fit your hands properly.

Then the cat clings precisely to your hand and bites it.

If we accept Salas’s definition of the hand as a means of action, the dream seems to be constantly asking:

“How am I going to handle this force? How am I going to act in response to it?”

And your behaviour is quite consistent:

you adjust the gloves;
you switch off the hair dryers;
you question your mother’s interpretation;
you observe the romantic drama from the outside;
you tear the cat away from your hand;
you keep looking for the right equipment.

It is not a dream in which you are being dragged along by events.

It is a dream in which you are continually calibrating your capacity to act.

My final reading

I would not interpret this dream as “you are going to have problems with women”.

That phrase appears inside the dream, not as a reliable external key, and it is not even documented as a symbol in the book.

What the dream does afterwards is explore the territory opened by that phrase: love, desire, rivalry, jealousy, aggression, kissing, biting, instinctive femininity. And it is especially striking that it ends up generating a black cat, which Salas does traditionally associate with that romantic sphere. But to me, that turns Part II into a kind of self-aware dream about how we manufacture interpretations, not into a prophecy.

And Luján had formulated the key before any of this happened:

what relationship exists between tango and fighting?

Probably a great deal.

Both require learning when to advance, when to retreat, how much space to leave the other person, when to respond, when to exert force and when to turn that force into coordination.

The same problem then appears in the mimes who kiss and bite one another, and finally in the cat that tries to take possession of your hand.

So, if I had to condense it into a single sentence:

it is a dream about learning to transform a force that is still poorly adjusted —affective, instinctive, creative or professional— into a form of action that is your own, controlled and mature.

And the fact that the dream ends by giving you a green tracksuit again and again seems to say that this process is still open: the identity is there, but it is still “green”.
