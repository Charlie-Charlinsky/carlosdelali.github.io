The Game

My work focused on creating systems for Superstar and live-service content. I designed Sphere of Influence, an RPG-inspired relationship and progression system in which connections with characters can change the course of the player's story. I later developed Showdown events built around distinct gameplay hooks, rules, objectives, and rewards.

Across both areas, the shared design challenge was to turn player actions into legible narrative consequences: relationships needed to react to decisions and gameplay, while live events had to transform a football theme or idea into a new way to play through a custom ruleset.

Contribution

Sphere of Influence

I designed Sphere of Influence as a relationship and progression system within Superstar. The feature connected what players did on and off the field with the characters around them, creating progression through influence, per-character relationship levels, and rewards tied to the trust earned with each character encountered throughout their career.

The system was structured around a clear causal loop: player actions and decisions affected the characters unlocked within their Sphere of Influence. Those relationships shifted through positive or negative Influence XP, and progression within each relationship changed the gameplay rewards available to the player. Rewards ranged from relationship-specific stat modifiers - such as better or worse catching and greater or lower speed on the field - to equipment upgrades for the player's avatar, decisions that altered the narrative, and a broad set of outcomes affecting the overall experience.

Relationship Progression & Rewards

My focus was to make relationships legible as a system rather than treat them as isolated narrative moments. Character progression, the rewards along each progression track, and gameplay-linked actions all had to reinforce one another so players understood why a relationship changed and what that change meant for their Superstar career.

This required designing rules, progression structures, and content relationships that could remain readable in the UI at scale and be sustained through data-driven pipelines.

Live Services - Showdown Events

Madden NFL 26 Live Services included an online competitive mode called Showdown.

I designed and developed Showdown events released throughout the year. Each event began with a gameplay hook or core idea that had to be translated into football rules, objectives, rewards, and pacing that kept competition clear and readable for players.

Beyond delivering the recurring event calendar, I developed new concepts and gameplay variations intended to expand the existing Showdown formula and keep the mode fresh throughout the live-service cycle. One of the most notable was the 11v11 game mode - the first 11v11 format created for Showdown.

Validation & Cross-Functional Delivery

The work depended on continuous alignment with engineering, production, and QV. Rules and progression logic had to be implemented through data and content pipelines, then tested and tuned against edge cases, technical constraints, and the intended player experience.

Madden NFL 26 Superstar became a strong example of my systemic approach at AAA scale: defining loops, exposing rules clearly, connecting dependencies, and iterating until a feature could be launched and sustained.
