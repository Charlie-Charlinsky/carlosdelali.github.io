The Game

In Madden NFL 27, I worked on the continued evolution of Superstar's systems before leaving EA SPORTS. My work spanned three major areas: Sphere of Influence, Battle Pass, and Side Activities. I was responsible for connecting them from different angles - player decisions, rewards, relationships, and long-term career progression.

The shared design goal across all three areas was consistency. Narrative decisions, seasonal rewards, and Side Activities could not operate as isolated content; they needed to function as connected systems that fed the player's career and Superstar's wider progression network. The metagame and on-field gameplay had to interweave as parts of a single system.

Contribution

Sphere of Influence - System Evolution

I continued developing and evolving Sphere of Influence content, expanding the relationship layer around player decisions, character reactions, conflicts, rewards, and long-term career progression.

The central goal was to strengthen the connection between narrative decisions and systemic consequences. Changes in relationships needed to affect the player's build and career progression in Superstar, making character interactions part of progression rather than narrative moments isolated from the rest of the game systems.

Battle Pass & Seasonal Progression

I worked on the Battle Pass architecture, defining how rewards and seasonal progression could support short-term goals without losing their connection to the long-term career loop. This was sustained by a Quest system, with daily and seasonal objectives that advanced players through the Battle Pass when completed.

The main task was to create a full-year plan comprising twelve seasons, with the Battle Pass acting as a content package that centralized rewards and connected the wider ecosystem, including Quests, objectives, Sphere of Influence, and even Showdown.

Each season had a distinct theme focused on specific positions on the field, such as QB, WR, or LB. This encouraged players to experiment with different positions and increased replayability through varied archetypes for each role.

Side Activities - Narrative Systems

Side Activities was another major part of my work. The system created encounters involving multiple characters from the player's Sphere of Influence. Each encounter appeared as a text pop-up presenting a situation to resolve by choosing one of the available responses. Depending on the choice, players could positively or negatively affect the characters involved, unlock new characters, or earn new rewards.

I designed Side Activities around plausible situations from the world of American football. The goal was to let players shape their relationships and wider narrative experience through decisions presented at the beginning of each new week throughout their career.

The full Side Activities plan was ultimately postponed to Madden NFL 28 because there was insufficient time and production capacity to implement it.

Content Architecture & Production Constraints

Across these systems, I worked within the realities of AAA production: cross-team dependencies, data-driven content, implementation capacity, and the need to create content structures that multiple disciplines could review, test, and expand.
