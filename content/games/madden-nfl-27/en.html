Overview

For Madden NFL 27, I worked on the evolution of Superstar systems before leaving EA SPORTS. My focus covered Sphere of Influence, Battle Pass and Side Activities, with each feature connecting player choices, rewards, relationships and longer-term career progression from a different angle.

The design goal across the three areas was consistency: narrative decisions, seasonal rewards and off-field activities should not behave as isolated content, but as connected systems feeding the same Superstar career and progression network.

My Contribution

Sphere of Influence — System Evolution

I continued evolving Sphere of Influence by expanding the relationship layer around player choices, character reactions, conflicts, rewards and longer-term career progression.

The central design objective was to strengthen the link between narrative decisions and systemic consequences. Relationship changes needed to affect the player's ongoing Superstar build and career, making character interactions part of progression rather than isolated story beats.

Battle Pass & Seasonal Progression

I worked on the Battle Pass structure for Madden NFL 27 Superstar, defining how seasonal rewards and progression could support short-term goals while remaining connected to the longer career loop.

The work focused on season structure, free and premium tracks, reward pacing and value, and the relationship between live-service content and the broader progression economy. The objective was for each season to feel distinct without becoming disconnected from Superstar progression.

Side Activities — Narrative Systems

Side Activities were another major part of my Madden NFL 27 work. I designed off-field activities around choices, character relationships, conflicts, rewards and progression, using them as smaller systemic situations that could feed back into the Superstar career.

The design challenge was to make each activity readable on its own while still contributing to the larger relationship and progression network. A player choice needed to create a character reaction, produce a relationship change or conflict, and ultimately connect back to career progression or rewards.

Content Architecture & Production Constraints

Across these systems, I worked within the realities of AAA production: existing foundations, cross-team dependencies, data-driven content, implementation capacity and the need for structures that could be reviewed, tested and expanded by multiple disciplines.

My contribution therefore combined system design with content architecture — turning high-level goals into authorable rules, reward structures and repeatable content patterns that engineering, production and QV could evaluate and support.
