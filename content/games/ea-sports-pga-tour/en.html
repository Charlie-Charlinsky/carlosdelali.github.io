Overview

EA SPORTS PGA TOUR was the project where my systems-design work expanded into a full AAA production cycle. I worked on the game for three years in a fully remote setup, operating with a high degree of autonomy across progression, content pipelines, live-service systems and player-facing gameplay features.

My contribution connected long-term golfer growth with repeatable content systems: quests and challenges, tournaments, Event Pass, XP and level progression, skill investment, ability unlocks and the gameplay/UI layer around ball striking.

My Contribution

Quests & Challenges

I helped deliver the system used to design and implement multiple types of quests and challenges. The workflow connected design, implementation and testing through a data-driven pipeline built around SQL, Excel and custom tools, allowing content to be authored consistently and validated before release.

The design challenge was not simply creating individual objectives, but building a structure that content designers could reuse across different quest types while keeping requirements, rewards and validation readable for the teams implementing and testing them.

Tournaments

I worked on the pipeline used to author tournaments across the live-service calendar, supporting daily, weekly and seasonal formats. The system had to expose the rules and configuration needed by content teams while remaining scalable enough to support repeated seasonal delivery.

This work reinforced my focus on content architecture: translating a recurring player-facing feature into a dependable authoring structure that other disciplines could populate, test and sustain over time.

Event Pass

I contributed to the Event Pass system used to centralise seasonal rewards and connect live-service activity with broader progression. The feature provided a structured place for rewards to be distributed through event-based progression while supporting different seasonal configurations.

The design work required alignment between reward pacing, player goals, progression state and the UI-facing presentation of the pass so the system felt connected to the rest of the game rather than operating as an isolated reward track.

Player Progression & Skill Tree

I owned the golfer level-progression structure and built the complete progression table and formulas used to balance XP, levels and long-term character growth. This work connected numerical progression curves with the pace at which players developed their golfer over time.

I also worked on the relationship between Skill Point investment, attributes and player abilities, helping ensure that growth choices translated into meaningful build development rather than a flat accumulation of numerical upgrades.

Skills, Abilities & Ball Striking

I expanded the golfer ability system by designing new ability unlocks around the existing Skill Points framework, linking progression choices to the way a player specialised their golfer.

Alongside progression, I designed gameplay- and UI-facing systems for ball striking, defining how new shot interactions behaved during play and how their state, feedback and available options were communicated to the player. This connected systemic design directly to moment-to-moment gameplay and interface readability.
